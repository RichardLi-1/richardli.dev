I sit in a sterile, white, mostly empty cube at work writing this. My MacBook rocks the default MacOS wallpaper and my lid devoid of stickers or any accessories. My neighbors fill their cubes with toy cars, rubber ducks, anything that they enjoy, visible and broadcast for all to see.

I'm no foreigner to this; when I first entered university, I put up a total of 2 photo strips and one letter over my bed, leading to its title of my "mental asylum." The first time I've ever put up those strips.

I've never been too open about things I've liked. There aren't that many "sensory" things I enjoy; I don't play video games, I don't watch shows or movies; sometimes hearing an entire crowd of seemingly, otherwise different people get together hyped up about a new anime leaves me confused. 

So it's possible that to me, the things I do enjoy are too precious; something that if too exposed someone can comment about and burn an immutable association in my head on it.

I still remember when my mother interrogated me for listening to "Like a Girl" by Lizzo in 2021; not a song I *particularly* liked, but now, I cannot listen without feeling some sort of sharp pain.

So if I share things I enjoy, I really trust you.

Sometimes I, or other people, remind me that I'm not truly devoid of joys, hobbies, or expression. Anyone who's met me, particularly when I was younger, knows I was an explosion of energy in real life. I love clubbing, I love EDM. I love working and feeling a sense of purpose. I used to love nerding out on new iPhone and other consumer electronic releases, tracking hurricanes and meteorological events like it's a full-time job, and creating different hypothetical variations of rockets using a code-naming and randomization system. But none of these interests and their associated activities have the privilege of being conveniently labelled with one, universally understandable word, unlike other things, like for example, "knitting." And so they don't get accepted as hobbies. If there's one thing about language and collective understand I've learned the hard way, it's that if something doesn't have a simple term, it's simply not very accepted by society. Easily accepted, at least. That's why we push.

It doesn't help that I really struggle to categorize my personality into a common trope, and that is a much bigger problem than it sounds. Gay engineer is an already uncommon combo, so is public transit-loving raver. I could go on.

I think this is a factor why I really struggled, at some points, to find peace in a small cohort environment like my university class of 100 people. It's so easy to forget who I am and what I enjoy, and it's nearly impossible to have the variety of people that let's me light up and express these different, completely unrelated parts of myself.

I think it's naturally difficult for people to understand the things I like, and so I've stopped seeking understanding. I don't bother to explain anymore.

I've opened up over time. The first times I sang my favourite songs in the car with my (then new) friend Fiona, I still remember. Some of my sister's friends, who I re-met at a wedding recently and who haven't seen me for years, say that my personality has changed. Outwardly, maybe it has. But the core, neural pathways I guess, of what causes this still remain.

So I guess that's why I haven't made this website too "personal," either. There's almost a pressure to make a brand identity of things you love; a common trend is the "floating stickers" of objects that are important to you or have inspired you.

I can't do that, as much as I've tried; adding that level of "personal" feels inauthentic. The real authentic experience is something that leaves room for interpretation, because as much as I love transit, pasting a sticker of my favourite train is too concrete. It would be unjust to the beauty and complexity of transit as a concept that makes it important. Most importantly, it would be unjust to who I am; and would be selling out the experiences that have shaped me that are too complex to be defined in a few words, a short description, a bedroom poster, or a section of a tech student's website.
